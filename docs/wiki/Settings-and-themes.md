**Settings:** click **Settings** at the foot of the nav, down the window's left side. What the app does comes first (**Files**, **Network**, the **Notes** font, the Map's accuracy note), then how it looks (**Theme**, **Emoji**, **Animate**), then the folder where settings and notes are kept. **Help wiki** at its top right opens this wiki.

**Network:**
- **Look up the public IP on start** (on by default): one DNS query to OpenDNS when the app opens, for the WAN address in the status bar. Off, it's looked up only on **Refresh** or a full scan.
- **Check for updates on start** (on by default): one HTTPS request to GitHub for the latest release when the app opens. A newer one shows as **Update: vX.Y.Z** at the right of the status bar and as a line here, both links to its release page, plus a short message. **Check now** checks at any time and says if you're up to date or why it couldn't check. Nothing is downloaded or installed: download the new `.exe` or `.AppImage` from the release page and replace the old file.

**Files:** **Save to** sets the folder every Save…, Export, Save as, the Site report and the Site Bundle start in (until one is chosen, the first Save… of a session asks for it; **Choose folder…** picks it here, **Reset** forgets it so the next Save… asks again). **A folder for each site** saves into a folder named after the open site inside it, made on the first save. The Save dialog still opens, so any file can go elsewhere.

**Portable mode:** make a folder named `is-alive-data` beside the program (the `.exe` or the `.AppImage` file) before starting it, and everything is-alive keeps (settings, sites, notes, saved maps, runs, `startup.log`) goes there, with nothing in the computer's profile. Remove the folder to go back to the profile. It's decided at start. **Remove everything is-alive saved…** at the bottom of Settings lists what's in the folder, asks, deletes it all and closes is-alive; files saved elsewhere with Save… or Export stay.

**MAC vendor table:** a line near the bottom of Settings gives the date of the vendor table built into the app and how many prefixes it holds. A weekly check against the IEEE registry opens a pull request when it changed, and the table comes with each release.

**Themes:** the theme list in **Settings** starts with the themes to work in: Dark (the default: graphite and blue, the one the app is designed in), Slate, Blueprint, Light, Paper and High contrast. The four showpieces, Phosphor, RGB (neon on black with a rainbow line under the tabs), Scanner and Miami, are folded under **More themes**. Every theme has the same controls: buttons, fields and choices the same shape and at least 28 points tall, the one main action of a screen (**Start**, **Full scan**) filled with the theme's accent. Each theme has its own Map colours (the menu shows them as swatches beside each name), and cards and links are tinted in their role's colour, with a soft glow on dark themes. The choice is remembered between launches (`%APPDATA%\is-alive\settings` on Windows, `~/.config/is-alive/settings` on Linux). Miami's own options show only while it's the theme picked. It puts the panels over a backdrop: a 16-bit synthwave scene drawn by the app (sunset, skyline, palms, a grid drifting toward you), your own picture with a slow pan (only its path is saved; the image never leaves your machine), or none. **Animate** loops the scene and the sunset line under the tabs and pauses while the window isn't focused. It's **On** (the default: everything that moves does so as smooth as the screen allows, on battery too), **When plugged in** (the battery-saving choice: on battery everything holds still, checked every half minute; a desktop always counts as plugged in) or **Off** (nothing fades, slides or flows). Tabs fade in, the nav folds and panels slide in over one of two lengths, about a tenth or a fifth of a second. **Glow** adds a soft halo and haze to the bright parts (the sun, neon and windows on the GPU; your picture's highlights either way). **VHS** (on by default, GPU only) plays the scene as if off a worn tape: faint scanlines, red and blue bleeding a hair apart, grain, lines jittering sideways, and every 9 seconds a tracking band rolling down the picture. The scene and a picture's pan run at 10 frames a second (the sunset line drifts at the screen's own rate), a slight chop that suits the look of a 16-bit scene off a VHS tape and keeps it cheap (the whole app redraws every frame; at the display's rate the Map tab took a third of a CPU core). On the GPU the scene is drawn at art resolution by a shader into an offscreen buffer and scaled up with hard edges, and a repaint between frames (a hover, typing) reuses that buffer. Without a usable GPU (a software OpenGL such as llvmpipe or Microsoft's Basic Render Driver, OpenGL below 3.1 / ES 3.0, or a shader the driver rejects) it's drawn on the CPU instead. `startup.log` says which is in use and why; `IS_ALIVE_GPU=off` forces the CPU.

Files named on the command line (or opened with **Open with → is-alive**) go straight to Config Export. The site that was open last opens as usual, its own configs read again first; the files named join them, and the site remembers them too.

**Ctrl+1 … Ctrl+9** switch to the first nine tabs and **Ctrl+0** to Results (Settings → **Keyboard and mouse** lists every shortcut; Speed Test and Subnet Calc, just before Results, have none), copies and saves confirm with a toast, and the window opens at the size it was closed at.

**The window.** The tools run down the left in a nav: open with their names when the window is 1100 points wide or more (on every tab, the Map too), folded to a rail of icons below that. Each tool's icon has a colour of its own in every theme, thirteen in all. **Fold** / **Open** at its foot keeps it the way you set it. **Settings** and **Help** are at the foot as well. Help opens the manual built into the app (**F1** opens it at the tab's chapter, in a window of its own beside is-alive or on another screen, without taking the keyboard from the app; its links work, and **Back in is-alive** puts it over the app instead) and this wiki. The title bar holds the site switcher (with **Site Bundle…** in its menu), **Report…** and **Full scan** (ping and port-check the gateway, trace to the internet, test DNS, sweep the LAN; Results opens to show them). The status bar shows this machine's LAN, gateway and WAN address, **Refresh** (re-read LAN and gateway, look up the public IP), what's running, the version and, when one is out, the **Update** link.
