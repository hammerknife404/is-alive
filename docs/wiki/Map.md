The **Map** tab draws one picture from what the app has seen, in the spirit of `/mapnetwork`: a tree from the internet down, roles as colours (gateway, switches, access points, servers/NAS, printers/cameras, clients, unidentified), infrastructure first and clients only on request.

**Controls** float over the map's top edge, so the map gets the whole window. On the left: **Find**, the layout (**Sites**, **Cluster**, **Tree**, **Zones**; one drop-down on a laptop-sized window or with Layers open), **Scan**, undo and redo, the findings (*⚠ 2 high · 4 med*, which opens **Layers**) and **⋯** for **Clients**, **Re-layout**, **Reload**, **Compare**, **Examples**, **Saved maps**, **Copy findings** and **Clear map**. On the right: **Layers**, **Legend** (off until you turn it on) and **Export** (in **⋯** on a laptop-sized window or with Layers open).

- **From a UniFi backup** (loaded in Config Export) it draws the real topology: each device under the switch or AP it uplinks to, with the port, wired or wireless (mesh, bridges). An uplink MAC the backup doesn't describe becomes an *Unidentified upstream device* card; a SonicWall export loaded alongside becomes the gateway, matched by its interface MACs.
- **From a sweep alone** it can't see switch ports, so devices hang under the gateway on dotted *assumed* links (a `/mapnetwork` map's dashed *inferred* links come in the same way; only its `wifi` links are wireless), with roles inferred from vendor and open ports (marked *inferred*). **Scan this network** on the empty map runs a sweep and the map fills in as hosts answer.
- **Both**: the sweep is merged into a backup only when it found hardware the backup knows by MAC. A backup read at the office isn't mixed up with the office network; the subtitle says so.
- **Several backups and exports** (a client with two sites, each with its own UniFi backup and SonicWall report): all of them go on one map. A device or client in two backups (one controller backed up on two days) is drawn once, as the backup opened later has it. Each controller's sites get their own box; two sites of one name (both *Default*) take the part of their backup's file name that differs (*Default (north)*, *Default (south)*). Each firewall is a gateway card of its own under the internet, with what it heard; a settings export and a report of the same firewall are one card. A device hangs under the firewall whose interface MAC is its uplink, else the one whose LAN holds its address, else the one the other devices of its UniFi site plug into (two sites can both use 192.168.1.x), else the firewall opened last. With no firewall, each backup's UniFi gateway is the gateway for that controller; with two or more firewalls and no UniFi sites, each firewall's part of the map is a site named after it.
- **From a /mapnetwork map**: open the `.dc.html` that `/mapnetwork` made (or a zip, such as the site bundle, you saved it into) in Config Export and the Map tab redraws it in this app's style. Only the map's data block is read, as plain values; the page's code never runs. Roles come from each device's kind (firewall, switch, AP, server…), the map's own zone, site and VLANs become details and chips, each device hangs off the first link that reaches it from the internet (any other link is listed in its details, and a device with no link up is drawn as *assumed*), and its findings fill the band. While a map is loaded it *is* the map: a SonicWall export loaded too is left out, a UniFi backup of the same site adds the clients the map left out (each backup on its own; one of another site stays out), and a sweep joins only when it found hardware the map knows by MAC, marking which devices answered.
- **⋯ › Clients** (off by default) puts end devices on the map, up to 10 under each switch or AP and the rest in one *+N more* card, within a 120-card budget; off, each uplink shows a count and the summary says how many clients are hidden. On a map with no end devices at all (an infrastructure-only `/mapnetwork` map) the switch is greyed out.
- **Examples:** **⋯ › Examples** (or the links on the empty map) opens two fictional sites drawn by `/mapnetwork`: *Acme Distribution* (one campus, HA firewalls, dual WAN) and *Contoso Health* (23 clinics, 81 devices). The one showing is ticked; pick another to switch, or **Close the example** to go back to this network. A sweep starting (**Scan**, the Subnet Sweep tab or **Full scan**) closes an example too, so it doesn't hide what the sweep finds. Opening any map or file again in Config Export makes it the one shown.
- **Findings**, worst first, only from evidence: telnet open, management only over plain HTTP, firewall management allowed on a WAN interface, devices UniFi lists as each other's uplink, unidentified uplinks, end-of-life models, mixed firmware on one model, devices in the swept range that didn't answer, and Ubiquiti hardware the backup doesn't know. They're listed in **Layers**, which the findings chip in the toolbar opens; click one to show its devices; **⋯ › Copy findings** for a ticket.

A note above the map says it's built only from what the app can see and points to Site Bundle → /mapnetwork in Claude for an accurate one; its **×** closes it for good (**Show the accuracy note** in Settings brings it back).

Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click the background to fit (or **Fit** in the corner; **Center** glides the middle of the map back to the middle of the view without changing the zoom), drag a card to move it (**⋯ › Re-layout** puts them all back; a moved card's link leaves from the side facing its uplink, as they sit), and click a card to open it in the **inspector** on the right, the same one a device opens in on Subnet Sweep: its role and name, address, zone and tags; **Ping**, **Port Check** and **Traceroute** (the result shows under **Results**), **Web**, **SSH** and **RDP** when they're open; action items, details, open ports with what each is for, its note and its connections. **⋯** at its top is the card's right-click menu (Copy details, Wake and the rest). From 1440 points wide the inspector sits beside the map; on a laptop it floats over the map's right edge, so the map keeps its size and zoom, and Layers, Legend and the zoom controls move beside it (into **⋯** on the smallest windows). **Esc** or **×** lets the card go. Opening **Layers** on a laptop shuts the floating inspector, the card still selected; pick a card or shut Layers to bring it back.

**What lights up:** one selected card lights its uplink and the links to the cards under it, with dots flowing away from the internet. Several selected cards light only the links that join them: two APs on one switch light their two links and that switch, an AP and the switch above it light the one link between them, and nothing past the topmost selected card lights up. **Trace to WAN** in the inspector's header (off by default) lights each selected card's whole path to the internet instead.

**Ctrl+click** (⌘ on a Mac) adds cards to the selection or takes them out: dragging one moves them all, and **Copy all** copies them. **Right-click** a card for *Select with its uplink*, *Select the path to the WAN*, *Select with its direct children* and *Select with everything below* (each shows only when it applies, with how many cards it takes), as well as Pin, Collapse or expand its site, and Copy details. **📌 Pin** a card (in its inspector, or right-click it on the map) to keep it selected while you click others; Esc and plain clicks leave pinned cards be, a pinned card holds its place in the inspector as others come and go (drag its grip to move it), and the map marks them with the thumbtack in the current emoji style. **Cards** keep their text readable at any zoom: zoomed out, a card drops lines (its kind, then chips, then the address) rather than shrinking them, and always shows its name; fitted far out (a big site at 20–30%), the name shows in a smaller type, and a site's name sits just above its box. Only a card too small even for that is a plain colour tile (hover names it). A link's port or note (*port 24*, *inferred*) is a chip on the card it leads to. Search matches name, IP, MAC and vendor; click a role in the legend to fade it out. **Export SVG** saves the map with its findings as a picture for a ticket or site doc.

**Clients** (end devices): **Off** puts a count on each switch or AP; **Some** shows up to 10 under each (fewer on a big map) and folds the rest into a **+N more** card, whose **Show all N** gives each of them a card (**Fold clients** on the switch or AP puts them back); **All** gives every client a card, however many there are (a big site can mean hundreds: **Zones** keeps them tidy).

**Layouts**, as `/mapnetwork` has them (the choice is remembered): **Cluster** (the default: a tree with end devices stacked in columns; when the level under the internet is much wider than it is tall, such as many sites each with their own firewall, it wraps into rows shaped like the window, and the rows below the first take their link down a line on the left edge so none crosses a row of cards), **Tree** (one row per level, every card side by side; can get wide), **Zones** (one box per role; links from one device into a box fold into one line with a ×N count, and links inside a box show when you select one end) and **Sites** (one box per site, each holding its part of the tree; the default when the map has two or more sites).

**Sites:** every map has them. A `/mapnetwork` map's come from its SITES (one without SITES is one site, named after the map), a multi-site UniFi controller backup (or several controllers' backups) puts each device at its own site, several firewalls with no UniFi sites are a site each, and anything else is one site: the controller's site name, the firewall's name, or *This network*. Click a site's title on the map (▾) to collapse it to one card, which hangs where the site does, lists what's inside, carries its flags and takes everything below the site; click again (▸) to open it. **Layers**' **Sites** list has the same toggle on each site, plus **Collapse all** and **Expand all**; click a site's name to select its cards (lighting the whole group's connections) and bring its box into view. Right-click a card to collapse or expand its site. Collapsed sites stay collapsed through rebuilds and in every layout, and a pinned card inside one keeps its pin for when the site opens.

**Updating:** the map rebuilds by itself whenever what it's drawn from changes: as a sweep finds hosts, when a backup, export or map is loaded (or loaded again) in Config Export, and on a host-info refresh. **⋯ › Reload** reads the map's files from disk again (every UniFi backup and SonicWall export open, and the `/mapnetwork` map in use) and rebuilds, for when a file changed after it was loaded. Rebuilds keep pins, the selection, the inspector's order, moved cards and card results. A pinned card that's no longer in the data isn't dropped: it stays pinned, drawn in red with a dashed outline and marked *Not in the latest data*, so you can check it (its Ping, Ports and Trace still work) before unpinning it or taking it out with ×.

The panels follow `/mapnetwork`'s, and neither changes the map's zoom: they lie over its edges (or, on a wide window, beside it), and a selected card is moved clear of them. On the left, **Layers** (shut until you open it), 272 points wide: the map's summary, the **sites** (see above), the **roles** with counts (click one to fade it out) and the **action items**, worst first (click one to show its devices); **×** shuts it, and so does **Esc** on a window under 1100 points wide once no cards are selected. From 1440 points wide it sits flush with the edges; below that it floats with a shadow, and the toolbar and legend move beside it. On a window too small for Layers and a card's inspector side by side, picking a card shuts Layers. On the right, the **inspector** (above): each selected card stacks, newest on top, with **Action items** (its flags and the findings that name it), **Details**, **Open ports**, **Results**, its **Note** and **Connections** (each a link to that card). **Ping**, **Port Check** and **Traceroute** run a quick check right in the card; each check's condensed result (`4/4 replies · avg 0.34 ms · 0% loss`, `Open: 22, 80, 443 · 12 closed`, `3 hops · 1.2 ms to 10.0.0.251`) shows under **Results**; click it for every reply, port or hop, **⟲** to run it again, **■** to stop it, **×** to clear it, or **Open in tab** for a full run in that tool's tab. Up to three checks run at once and the rest wait their turn. They're spot checks: they stay with the card for the session but don't go to the Results tab or exports. × takes a card out of the stack; drag a card's grip (⠿ at its top left) to move it up or down. Cards glide to their new places when the stack changes. **Fit** (or **F**, or a double-click on the background) frames the whole map in the room the open panels leave and keeps it there as the map grows; with every panel shut it fills the window again. While a **Scan** runs, a progress bar under the toolbar shows hosts probed, rate and time left. On a laptop-sized window, or with Layers open, the layouts fold into one drop-down and **Export** moves into **⋯**.

## The /mapnetwork round trip

1. Sweep the site, load its UniFi / SonicWall / Meraki configs in Config Export (every controller and firewall the visit covers), and make a [[Site Bundle|Site-Bundle-and-Claude]].
2. In Claude, attach the zip and paste the kickoff prompt: `/unifidump` and `/mapnetwork` build the accurate map as a `.dc.html`.
3. Open that `.dc.html` in Config Export: the Map redraws it in this app's style, and a sweep of the same network marks which devices answer. On the next visit, include it in the bundle and `/mapnetwork` updates it instead of starting over.
