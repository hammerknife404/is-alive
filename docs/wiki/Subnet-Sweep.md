Ping sweep of a CIDR or range (`10.0.0.0/24`, `10.0.0.1-254`, `10.0.0.10-10.0.1.20`) with hostnames, MAC and vendor, ping-silent devices from the ARP table, optional management-port check, and an **Inventory** JSON for /mapnetwork.

Like every tab: the run bar (the main field first, **▶ Start** right after it), the other settings folded under **Options** with their values shown as chips, a status row under it with what the run found on the left and **Copy** / **Save…** on the right, and a short explanation of what to do before the first run. Enter in the main field starts a run; Esc stops it.

Each live host's **MAC** comes from this machine's ARP table (Windows `GetIpNetTable2`, Linux `/proc/net/arp`; no admin rights), so only hosts on your own segment have one. **Vendor** is the IEEE registry owner of the MAC prefix (MA-L, MA-M and MA-S, bundled offline; rebuild with `python3 scripts/update-oui.py`). A locally administered MAC shows as *private/virtual*: a phone or laptop's per-network private address, or a VM/container NIC.

**Ping-silent devices.** Pinging an address on your own segment makes the OS send an ARP request first, and almost nothing refuses ARP. Hosts that answer ARP but drop ping (Windows firewall defaults, many IoT devices and cameras) are listed with *no ping* in the Time column and counted as *ARP only* in the header. Only entries the OS has just confirmed count (Linux `ip neigh` REACHABLE, Windows Reachable), so devices that left minutes ago don't show up. The sweep reads the table every 3 s while it runs. Past a router there is no ARP, so only ping counts there. On Linux this needs `ip` (iproute2); without it the sweep is ping-only. The Inventory marks each host `found_by` `ping` or `arp`.

**Mgmt ports** adds a TCP connect once the sweep has finished, on 22, 23, 80, 443, 445, 541 (FortiGate), 554 (RTSP), 3389, 4433 (SonicWall), 5000, 8080/8443 (UniFi), 8291 (MikroTik) and 9100 (printers), at the same speed limit. Open management ports are the evidence for telling switches, APs and firewalls from clients. **Smart** (the default) checks the gateway, network gear, NAS, printers, cameras and devices from unknown makers, and skips what's clearly someone's phone or laptop (a private MAC, or a laptop or phone network card's maker such as Intel, Realtek, Apple or Samsung); **All hosts** checks every live host; **Off** checks none. Full scan uses the same choice. Every host the sweep found stays listed whether or not its ports were checked; a skipped one reads *not checked*, and Compare (Results and the Map) doesn't count it as closed.

**A device's details.** Click a row to open the host in the **inspector** on the right: its kind (gateway, this machine, ARP only), name and address, MAC, vendor, where the name came from (DNS, NetBIOS or multicast DNS), ping and TTL, and its open ports when they were checked. **Ping**, **Ports** and **Trace** run that tool against it in its tab, **Web** opens its page (https, or http/8443 from its open ports) and **On map** selects its card on the Map. From 1100 points wide the inspector sits beside the table; below that it floats over the table and **Esc** closes it. As the table narrows, **Vendor** drops out first, then **MAC**; both stay in the inspector.

**Inventory** saves the run as JSON for `/mapnetwork`'s scan input: this machine's LAN, gateway and WAN, the ports checked, and per host `ip`, `mac`, `vendor`, `private_mac`, `name`, `rtt_ms`, `ttl`, `open_ports`, `is_gateway` and `is_this_machine`. Facts only, no role guesses; the MACs are what join it to `/unifidump`'s `discovered_devices` and uplink MACs.

The **Speed** preset works as in [Port Check](Port-Check#scan-speed).

**CSV columns** (Results → Export → CSV): range, ip, rtt_ms, ttl, mac, vendor, name, open_ports, found_by (alive hosts; `arp` rows have no rtt_ms). See [[Results]].
