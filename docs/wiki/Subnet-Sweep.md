Ping sweep of a CIDR or range (`10.0.0.0/24`, `10.0.0.1-254`, `10.0.0.10-10.0.1.20`) with hostnames, MAC and vendor, ping-silent devices from the ARP table, optional management-port check, and an **Inventory** JSON for /mapnetwork.

Like every tab: the run bar (the main field first, **▶ Start** right after it), the other settings folded under **Options** with their values shown as chips, a status row under it with what the run found on the left and **Copy** / **Save…** on the right, and a short explanation of what to do before the first run. Enter in the main field starts a run; Esc stops it.

Each live host's **MAC** comes from this machine's ARP table (Windows `GetIpNetTable2`, Linux `/proc/net/arp`; no admin rights), so only hosts on your own segment have one. **Vendor** is the IEEE registry owner of the MAC prefix (MA-L, MA-M and MA-S, bundled offline; rebuild with `python3 scripts/update-oui.py`). A locally administered MAC shows as *private/virtual*: a phone or laptop's per-network private address, or a VM/container NIC. The exception is gear that makes a virtual MAC from its own (a SonicWall's LAN answers with its interface MAC with that bit set): on the gateway, or on a host showing gear of its own (telnet, 541, 4433 or 8291 open, or a gear web page) whose MAC with the bit cleared is a network-gear maker's, Vendor reads *SonicWall (virtual MAC)* and the host counts as gear. The gateway is never private. HP's prefixes cover printers, PCs and Poly phones, so the role comes from the page.

**Ping-silent devices.** Pinging an address on your own segment makes the OS send an ARP request first, and almost nothing refuses ARP. Hosts that answer ARP but drop ping (Windows firewall defaults, many IoT devices and cameras) are listed with *no ping* in the Time column and counted as *ARP only* in the header. Only entries the OS has just confirmed count (Linux `ip neigh` REACHABLE, Windows Reachable), so devices that left minutes ago don't show up. The sweep reads the table every 3 s while it runs. Past a router there is no ARP, so only ping counts there. On Linux this needs `ip` (iproute2); without it the sweep is ping-only. The Inventory lists how each host was found in `found_by` (`ping`, `arp`, `mdns`, `ssdp`).

**Mgmt ports** adds a TCP connect once the sweep has finished, on 21 (FTP, often left on in printers and NAS), 22, 23, 80, 443, 445, 541 (FortiGate), 554 (RTSP), 3389, 4433 (SonicWall), 5000, 8080/8443 (UniFi), 8291 (MikroTik) and 9100 (printers), at the same speed limit. Open management ports are the evidence for telling switches, APs and firewalls from clients. **Smart** (the default) checks the gateway, network gear, NAS, printers, cameras and devices from unknown makers, and skips what's clearly someone's phone or laptop (a private MAC, or a laptop or phone network card's maker such as Intel, Realtek, Apple or Samsung); **All hosts** checks every live host; **Off** checks none. Full scan uses the same choice. Every host the sweep found stays listed whether or not its ports were checked; a skipped one reads *not checked* (*checking…* while its ports are being tried), and Compare (Results and the Map) doesn't count it as closed. A sweep stopped part way says so in its report and in the Inventory (`complete: false`), and hosts whose ports it hadn't finished read *not checked*.

**Web page.** After the port check, each device with a web port open (80, 443, 4433, 5000, 8080, 8443) gets one GET of its own page: its `<title>`, else its `Server` header (*UniFi OS*, *SonicWall - Authentication*, an HP printer's page). Read-only, no login, HTTPS first, two ports at most and one redirect on the device itself. It shows in the inspector and the report, goes into the inventory as `web_title`, and places a device of an unknown maker on the Map (NAS, printer, camera, server, or gear by brand).

**DNS-SD.** With names on and the sweep covering part of this computer's own segment, one DNS-SD browse goes out as the sweep starts: a single multicast DNS packet to 224.0.0.251:5353 asking for `_services._dns-sd._udp.local`, the printer types (`_ipp._tcp`, `_ipps._tcp`, `_pdl-datastream._tcp`, `_printer._tcp`), file shares (`_smb._tcp`) and web pages (`_http._tcp`), with the unicast-response (QU) bit set, sent from an ephemeral port so devices answer straight back to it. Nothing binds or listens on 5353, and nothing is sent to a printer's 9100 (no PJL). Answers are read after the ping and ARP phases: each device's service types, and from a printer's TXT record its model (`ty=`, `product=`), its queues (`rp=`, such as `ipp/print`) and the page languages it takes (`pdl=`, such as `application/pdf`). A sleep proxy answering for a sleeping printer is credited to the printer's own address when that's on the scanner's segment. A model belongs to the printer service, so a computer or print server sharing a printer shows that printer's model (none if it shares several different ones). The read stops at most 250 ms after its 2.5 s wait and after 1,024 answers, however much keeps arriving. The inspector shows them as **Model**, **Queue**, **Languages** and **Services**, the report counts who answered, and the inventory has them as `dnssd` (`services`, `ty`, `product`, `rp`, `pdl`) with `dnssd_browsed` at the top.

**SSDP.** At the same moment, one SSDP M-SEARCH (`ST: ssdp:all`, `MX: 2`) goes to 239.255.255.250:1900 from an ephemeral port with TTL 1 (so no router, multicast router or IGMP proxy passes it on), and devices reply straight back to it. Nothing binds or listens on 1900, so NOTIFY announcements aren't heard, and the `LOCATION` URL a device gives is kept as text and **never fetched**. From each reply it keeps `SERVER` (the device's OS and UPnP stack, often its product), the `ST` it answered for (its device and service types) and the UUID in `USN`. The same limits as DNS-SD hold: a 2.5 s wait, at most 250 ms more and 1,024 replies, and replies that are cut short or malformed are skipped, never a crash. The inspector shows **UPnP** (the server) and **UPnP types** (device types such as *MediaRenderer*), the report adds the server, and the inventory has `ssdp` (`server`, `st`, `uuids`, `locations`) with `ssdp_searched` at the top.

**Found by multicast.** A device that answered the DNS-SD browse or the SSDP search but not ping or ARP (cast targets, TVs, speakers and IoT gear often are) is **added** to the results, as long as the answer came from the device's own address, inside the swept range and on this computer's own segment. An address merely named in another device's answer (a sleep proxy's, say) can label a device the sweep found anyway, but never adds one. A sweep stopped before the answers were read says nothing about DNS-SD or SSDP. Its **Time** says *no ping*, the status row counts it as *multicast only*, its inspector says *Host · mDNS only* (or SSDP, or both) with a note, and the Map shows it with a *Multicast only* chip. Every host says how it was found: **Found by** in the inspector, *(found by ping, arp, mdns)* at the end of its report line, `found_by` in the inventory and the CSV. Multicast doesn't cross routers, so only the scanner's own segment can answer or be added this way: each place it's shown says so.

**Wake.** The inspector's **Wake** button, or **Wake (Wake-on-LAN)** on a row's right-click menu, sends a host with a real MAC the same Wake-on-LAN packet as a Map card. A host inside a range the sweep covered whole (a /22 to /30) also gets that range's broadcast, which only helps if the range is its real subnet and the router forwards directed broadcasts.

**SNMP.** The inspector's **SNMP** button asks the picked device, and only it, over SNMP v2c (then v1) on UDP 161: its system group (`sysName`, `sysDescr`, `sysObjectID` with the maker it names, `sysUpTime`, `sysLocation`, and `sysContact` on screen only, never in Results, the report or the bundle), ENTITY-MIB's first chassis (`entPhysicalModelName`, `FirmwareRev` or else `SoftwareRev`, each labelled as what it is, `SerialNum`; the walk stops at that entry), on a printer `hrPrinterStatus`, `hrPrinterDetectedErrorState` (decoded: *low toner*, *jammed* …) and its supplies with what's left, and the LLDP neighbour table (its own port, the far end's `lldpRemSysName`, `lldpRemPortId` and `lldpRemPortDesc`): the switch port a printer or AP is on. An object ID of Net-SNMP or Broadcom FASTPATH is shown as **Agent software**, not a maker: many makers' devices run them. Each request waits 2 s and is sent twice (an answer to either counts), the whole read stops at 30 s and a **Note** says what was cut short and what wasn't read, LLDP and ENTITY-MIB walks stop at 64 rows and supplies at 32, and answers count only from the device's address and port 161, matched by request ID. The reading shows in an **SNMP** section under the buttons, with the **Community** (shown as dots, `public` until changed, in memory for the session only) and **Read again**, and goes to Results as an **SNMP** run. No answer is said as such: SNMP off, a wrong community, an ACL by source IP and a v3-only agent all look the same, so it doesn't guess. Never part of a sweep or Full scan.

**Duplicate IPs.** The sweep keeps every MAC each address answered from (fresh or confirmed ARP entries only). One that answered from two is flagged in the inspector (*Also from*), the report, the inventory (`other_macs`) and on the Map as a finding: two devices on one address, or a failover mid-sweep.

**Self-assigned addresses.** A host on 169.254.x.x picked its own address because DHCP didn't answer it. The report says how many (*⚠ 1 self-assigned address(es)*), marks the host's line, and the Map makes it a finding: check the DHCP server or scope and the switch port.

**A device's details.** Click a row to open the host in the **inspector** on the right: its kind (gateway, this machine, ARP only, mDNS or SSDP only), how it was found, name and address, MAC, vendor, where the name came from (DNS, NetBIOS or multicast DNS), ping and TTL, and its open ports when they were checked. **Ping**, **Ports** and **Trace** run that tool against it in its tab, **Web** opens its page (https, or http/8443 from its open ports), **On map** selects its card on the Map, **Domain join** puts it in Domain join's box, **Wake** sends it Wake-on-LAN and **SNMP** reads it over SNMP (above). From 1100 points wide the inspector sits beside the table; below that it floats over the table and **Esc** closes it. As the table narrows, **Vendor** drops out first, then **MAC**, then **TTL**; all stay in the inspector.

**Inventory** saves the run as JSON for `/mapnetwork`'s scan input: this machine's LAN, gateway and WAN, the ports checked, and per host `ip`, `mac`, `vendor`, `private_mac`, `virtual_mac` (a locally administered MAC that is the gateway's or a gear maker's virtual one; `vendor` then from the MAC with that bit cleared), `name`, `found_by` (a list: `ping`, `arp`, `mdns`, `ssdp`), `rtt_ms`, `ttl`, `open_ports`, `web_title`, `dnssd`, `ssdp`, `is_gateway`, `is_this_machine` and `on_scanner_lan`. Facts only, no role guesses; the MACs are what join it to `/unifidump`'s `discovered_devices` and uplink MACs.

The **Speed** preset works as in [Port Check](Port-Check#scan-speed).

**CSV columns** (Results → Export → CSV): range, ip, rtt_ms, ttl, mac, vendor, name, open_ports, found_by (every way the host answered, space-separated: `ping`, `arp`, `mdns`, `ssdp`; rows without `ping` have no rtt_ms). See [[Results]].
